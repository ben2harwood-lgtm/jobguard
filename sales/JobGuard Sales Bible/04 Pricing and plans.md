# JobGuard Sales Bible — Part 4: Pricing and plans

This is part 4 of the JobGuard Sales Bible. It covers what JobGuard costs, how the monthly plans work, how jobs are counted, and how to explain the price. All prices exclude VAT. These are the launch prices; Ben confirms the price list, so always quote from the current list.

## The whole price in four lines

1. **Quoting is free, forever.** Builders can quote as many jobs as they like without paying.
2. **A monthly plan, based on how many jobs you've got on the go at once.** From £29 a month. Unlimited users on every plan.
3. **Your first live job is free.**
4. **A 10% fee only on extra money JobGuard finds or recovers for you, and only once you've been paid.** (See Part 3.)

## How jobs are counted — the pricing-page sentence

Learn this word for word:

> "We count the jobs you've got on the go, not jobs you've ever created. A job starts counting when work starts on site, or 30 days after you switch it live, whichever comes first. It stops when you complete the final account, a week after you mark it finished on site, or once it has gone quiet for a month. Small jobs (under £2,000 when accepted) don't take a place, up to your plan's number of them at once; if a small job grows past £4,000 before you complete the account, it takes a place from then. Your first job is free."

What that means in practice:

- **Quotes never count.** Quote a hundred jobs; they're free.
- **Won jobs that haven't started don't count** for up to 30 days. Builders can switch a job live as soon as it's won, so JobGuard can check the orders and plan the start, without paying for weeks before they're on site.
- **A job counts while the builder is working on it.** It starts counting at the first sign of work on site (a site photo, a delivery, a diary note) or 30 days after switching live.
- **A job stops counting** when the final account is completed, when the builder marks it "Finished on site" (a week later), after a month with no activity, or if the builder closes it.
- **Snagging, invoicing, waiting for payment and retention never count.** Once the work's done, the job is off the meter.
- **Small jobs are free.** A job worth under £2,000 when accepted doesn't take a place — a boiler swap or a small repair shouldn't cost a place. If it grows past £4,000 before the final account is completed, it starts counting.
- **Going over is never blocked.** If a builder goes one job over their plan, that job costs the extra-job price, only for the days it's on. When it's cheaper to move up a plan, JobGuard says so.

## The free first job

- A builder's first live job costs nothing until they complete its final account, or for 90 days, whichever comes first — as long as it's their only live job.
- JobGuard asks for a card when they switch that first job live (with a "skip for now" option). The card has to be on file before a second job goes live.
- The Final Check and the 10% work normally on the free job, so the builder experiences the real thing.
- One free first job per business.

**How to use it:** "Don't take my word for it. Put your next job on JobGuard. It costs nothing until that job's finished. See what it catches."

Builders will compare JobGuard with the apps they know. Typical UK prices, excluding VAT:

| Product | What it costs | How it charges |
|---|---|---|
| Tradify | £34–£44 | per user, per month |
| Powered Now | £28–£40 | per user, per month |
| Fergus | £39–£45 | per month (sources differ on whether per user) |
| YourTradebase | £29 plus £15 per extra user | per month |
| ServiceM8 | £25–£119 (more for big volumes) | by number of jobs a month, unlimited users |
| Checkatrade quoting tool | free | for Checkatrade members |
| Voice-quote apps (Qted, VoxTrade and others) | £0–£59 | per month |

**The comparison to make:** a five-person firm on a per-user app pays five times the price. On Tradify at £37 a user that's about £185 a month. On JobGuard Builder it's £69 for the whole team, and JobGuard checks supplier invoices, builds the final account and runs the Final Check, which none of those apps do.

Don't knock the other apps. Say: "They're good at booking and invoicing jobs. JobGuard is built for the thing they don't do: making sure every penny you're owed ends up on the bill and gets paid."

## The 10% fee (summary — full detail in Part 3)

- 10% of money the Final Check finds that the builder then bills and gets paid for.
- 10% of a cash refund from a supplier the builder had already overpaid.
- 10% of money recovered through a recovery case the builder chose to open and agreed the fee for.
- Never on anything the builder logged themselves, never on money not yet received, never on ordinary late payments, never on overcharges caught before paying.
- Shown on the same screen as the customer's payment, collected by Direct Debit, plus VAT.
- **The monthly plan and the 10% are separate.** The plan is never knocked off the fee, and the fee is never knocked off the plan.

## The builder is always in control of the bill

- **A fixed monthly maximum.** Every plan has a maximum it can cost in a month (the last column in the plans table). The 10% fee is separate and only arises on money you've been paid. It can't go over that without the builder's say-so.
- **The bill is flat unless you went over.** A normal month shows one line. Only a month with extra jobs lists which jobs and how many days.
- **Month to month.** Monthly plans are designed to run month to month; check the current terms for cancellation. Moving down a plan takes effect at the next billing date; moving up is immediate.
- **Every fee is shown before it's collected.**

## How to talk about price

- **Lead with the leaks, not the price.** Once a builder has told you about the extras they forgot and the invoice they never checked, £29 or £69 a month is small.
- **Say the price plainly and early.** Builders hate a salesperson who dodges the price. "It's £29 a month for up to four jobs on the go, quoting's free, and your first job's free."
- **Explain the 10% in the same breath.** "And if our Final Check finds money you forgot to bill, we take 10% of it, only once you're paid. Anything you log yourself is always 100% yours."
- **Say "about a pound a day" for Solo.** It's true, and builders relate to it.
- **Don't discount.** A fair price, stated plainly, signals a serious product. If a builder is unsure, the free first job is the answer, not a discount.
- **Don't promise savings figures.** Let the builder's own experience do the maths.
