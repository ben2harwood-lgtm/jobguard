# JobGuard Sales Bible — Part 2: How JobGuard saves builders money

This is part 2 of the JobGuard Sales Bible. It explains, leak by leak, where builders lose money and how JobGuard stops it, with worked examples Tommy can talk through.

**A rule before anything else:** the examples below are illustrations of how the product works, not promises. Never tell a builder "JobGuard will save you £X". Say "here's how it catches this kind of thing" and let them put their own numbers on it. The builder always knows their own leaks better than we do.

## The facts Tommy can quote

These come from published UK sources and are safe to use:

- **Late payment costs the UK economy about £11 billion a year** and causes about **14,000 business closures a year**. At any one time about **£26 billion** is owed to small businesses. (UK government research published by the Small Business Commissioner, July 2025.)
- **About a third of tradespeople (34%) say they don't get paid for extra work** they do. (Simply Business survey of 408 UK tradespeople. It's one survey — say "one survey found", don't present it as an official figure.)
- **Construction is the least digital trade in the UK.** Only 29% of one-person construction businesses use web-based software to run the business, against 53% across all sectors. (UK government Longitudinal Small Business Survey 2024.)
- **Making Tax Digital is forcing software on sole traders.** From April 2026, sole traders earning over £50,000 must keep digital records; that drops to £30,000 in April 2027 and £20,000 in April 2028.

Don't use made-up statistics such as "builders lose £X a year to unbilled work" — there's no reliable UK figure, and a sharp builder will ask where it came from.

## Leak by leak

### 1. Things missed on the site visit

**What happens:** The builder walks round, makes notes on a scrap of paper or in their head, and types up the quote that evening. Something gets forgotten: the skip, the making good, the extra radiator valve, moving the boiler flue. Either they do it for free or they have an awkward conversation later.

**What JobGuard does:** The builder talks through the job on site. JobGuard turns it into a full list, with each line linked back to what was said, and flags gaps before the quote goes out — for example, work mentioned with no matching line, or a line with no quantity. It can also flag property issues at quote time, such as a listed building or conservation area, before the builder commits to a price.

**How to say it:** "You walk round and talk. JobGuard writes it up and tells you what's missing before you send the quote, not after you've done the work."

### 2. Underpriced quotes

**What happens:** Lines priced from memory, old merchant prices, or no price at all ("I'll work that out later").

**What JobGuard does:** It suggests prices from the builder's own rates and past jobs, and learns their rates over time. Any line without a confirmed price is flagged; JobGuard never makes up a price. The builder always decides.

**How to say it:** "It uses your own prices, not someone else's. And it won't let a line go out with no price on it."

### 3. Materials: overcharges, short deliveries, double bills

**What happens:** The merchant agreed £1.20 a metre and invoiced £1.80. The delivery was 40 lengths short but the invoice charged for all of them. The same invoice turned up twice and both got paid. Most small builders never check — there isn't time.

**What JobGuard does:** Orders are checked against agreed prices before they're placed. When supplier invoices and delivery notes come in (photographed, forwarded, or straight from a connected merchant account), JobGuard matches three things: what was ordered, what was delivered, and what was invoiced. Anything that doesn't match goes into "Things to check", with the exact lines side by side.

**The key selling point:** most of these get caught **before the builder pays**, and those catches are **free** — they're part of the subscription. JobGuard only takes 10% if the builder has already paid and JobGuard helps get a cash refund.

**How to say it:** "When did you last check a merchant invoice line by line against the order and the delivery note? JobGuard does it every time, before you pay."

### 4. Site not ready

**What happens:** A crew of three turns up and the plasterboard isn't there, or the electrician hasn't finished first fix, or the screed isn't dry. That's a wasted day of wages.

**What JobGuard does:** Readiness warnings ahead of the day: materials not yet delivered, the previous stage not signed off, access not confirmed, drying time not up, weather.

**How to say it:** "It tells you on Tuesday that Thursday's not going to work, instead of your lads finding out on Thursday."

### 5. No proof before work is covered

**What happens:** The pipework gets boxed in, the wiring goes behind the boards, the steel goes behind the plaster, and nobody took a photo. Then the customer or building control asks, and it has to be opened up — or the customer disputes it.

**What JobGuard does:** Photo and certificate reminders tied to each stage ("first-fix wiring photos before boarding"). A stage can't be marked done without the right proof. Every photo is dated and filed against the job and the right part of the work.

**How to say it:** "Every photo you need, taken at the right time, filed against the right bit of the job. When there's an argument, you've got the evidence."

### 6. Extras never charged

**What happens:** "While you're here, can you just…" The builder says yes, does it, and never writes it down. By the time the bill goes out, it's forgotten. Or it's remembered, but the customer says "I thought that was included".

**What JobGuard does, in two layers:**

1. **Log an extra** — in seconds, by voice or typing. Price it, send it to the customer to approve on their phone, and it's on the final bill automatically. **Everything the builder logs is 100% theirs — JobGuard takes nothing.**
2. **The Final Check** — after the builder completes their final bill, JobGuard checks the job's receipts, photos, diary notes and customer messages for work that was done but isn't on the bill. If it finds any, it shows the builder with proof. If they bill it and get paid, JobGuard takes 10% of that. (Full detail in Part 3.)

**How to say it:** "Log it and it's all yours. Forget to log it, and our Final Check is your safety net — and we only take 10% if you actually get paid for it."

### 7. Slow bills and slow payers

**What happens:** The final bill goes out three weeks after the job finishes because the builder has to piece it together. Customers pay late. Some never pay the last bit.

**What JobGuard does:**
- The final account is ready the day the job finishes — no piecing together.
- Invoices go out with pay-now links (card or pay-by-bank), so customers can pay in seconds. Bank transfer is always available too.
- Deposits and stage payments can be collected as the job goes.
- Reminders are drafted for the builder to approve.
- If a customer won't pay, the builder can open a **recovery case**: JobGuard pulls together the evidence (accepted quote, approved extras, dated photos, messages) and drafts factual letters for the builder to send. If money is recovered, JobGuard's fee is 10% of what's recovered. If nothing is recovered, the builder pays nothing.

**How to say it:** "Your final bill's ready the day you finish, not three weeks later. And if someone won't pay, you've got every approval and photo in one pack."

### Bonus: disputes

A dispute costs time, stress, and often money. JobGuard's biggest protection here is simple: the customer approved every extra in writing, every stage has dated photos, and the whole job is in one checkable record. Most disputes never start when the paperwork is that clear.

## Worked example 1: a £30,000 extension

This is the example to learn by heart. It shows exactly what's free and what's 10%.

1. **Quote accepted:** £30,000.
2. **During the job** the customer asks for extras. The builder logs them with Log an extra: £3,200 in total. The customer approves each one on their phone. **JobGuard fee: £0.**
3. **At the end** JobGuard has built the final account: £30,000 + £3,200 = £33,200.
4. **Final review:** the builder remembers one more thing — £400 of extra patio work — and adds it. **JobGuard fee: £0.** Final account: £33,600.
5. **The builder presses "Complete final account".**
6. **Final Check:** JobGuard finds two things done but not billed:
   - An outside tap: the customer asked for it in a message, the builder bought the tap kit from the merchant, and a photo shows it fitted. Not in the quote, not in the extras, not in the final bill. Worth £500.
   - Two extra sockets in the utility: the builder's own diary note says "put two more sockets in the utility for Mrs Patel". Never logged as an extra. Worth £300.
7. **The builder confirms both**, the customer approves them, and they go on the same final invoice: £34,400 + VAT.
8. **The customer pays.**

**What the builder pays JobGuard for this job:**
- On the £3,600 of extras the builder logged or added themselves: **£0**.
- On the £800 the Final Check found: **£80** (10%, plus VAT). **The builder keeps £720** of money they would otherwise not have billed.
- Plus their normal monthly plan (for example £69 a month on the Builder plan, which covers up to 7 jobs at once).

## Worked example 2: a supplier overcharge

- The builder agreed £1.20 a metre for timber with the merchant. The invoice says £1.80 a metre on 400 metres: £240 too much.
- **If JobGuard catches it before the builder pays** (the normal case): the builder queries it, the merchant corrects it, the builder pays the right amount. **JobGuard fee: £0.** It's part of the subscription.
- **If the builder had already paid**, and JobGuard spots it afterwards: the builder asks for a refund and the merchant refunds £240 in cash. **JobGuard fee: £24** (10%). If the merchant only gives a credit note against future orders, there's no fee.

## Worked example 3: a customer who won't pay

- A customer owes the last £4,000 on a kitchen and has stopped answering.
- The builder opens a recovery case and agrees the 10% fee terms for that case.
- JobGuard builds the evidence pack (the accepted quote, approved extras, sign-off photos, the customer's own messages) and drafts factual, polite letters. The builder approves and sends them.
- The customer pays £4,000. **JobGuard fee: £400.** The builder gets £3,600 they might have written off.
- If the customer never pays: **JobGuard fee: £0.**
- Ordinary late payment — a customer who pays a few weeks late after the normal reminders — is **never** charged. The 10% only applies to a recovery case the builder chooses to open.

## Worked example 4: partial payment and refunds

- Of the £800 found in example 1, the customer pays half now and half next month. JobGuard's fee is £40 now and £40 next month — it follows the money.
- If the customer later gets a £200 refund on the extra work, the builder's paid amount drops to £600, JobGuard's fee drops to £60, and the £20 difference is credited back.

## The honest bit

Most of what JobGuard saves a builder is **prevention**: mistakes that never happen, overcharges caught before they're paid, extras logged at the time. Prevention is hard to put a number on — you can't prove the mistake would have happened. That's why the monthly plan exists: it pays for all that protection, and it doesn't change with how much JobGuard stops.

The 10% is only on money JobGuard can **prove** it found or recovered, after the fact, and only once it's actually in the builder's bank account.

When talking to a builder, lead with the leaks they recognise, not with numbers you can't back up. Ask them: "Which of these cost you money last year?" Then let them tell you how much.
