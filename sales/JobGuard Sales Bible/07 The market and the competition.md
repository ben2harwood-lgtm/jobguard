# JobGuard Sales Bible — Part 7: The market and the competition

This is part 7 of the JobGuard Sales Bible. It explains the UK builder software market, the products builders already use, and what makes JobGuard different. Competitor prices were checked in September 2026 and change often; don't quote a competitor's price to a builder as definite.

## The market in plain English

- **Most small builders still run on paper, WhatsApp and memory.** Only 29% of one-person construction firms use web-based software to run the business, against 53% of all small businesses (UK government survey, 2024). Construction is the least digital sector. That's JobGuard's opportunity: most prospects aren't switching from a rival, they're starting for the first time.
- **Making Tax Digital is pushing builders onto software.** Sole traders over £50,000 must keep digital records from April 2026, over £30,000 from April 2027 and over £20,000 from April 2028. Many builders are choosing software now.
- **Late payment is a huge, well-known problem**: about £11 billion a year to the UK economy and about 14,000 business closures a year (UK government research, 2025).
- **Builders already pay for apps.** The usual UK trade job app costs about £25–£45 per user per month.

## The kinds of products builders use

### 1. Trade job apps (the ones Tommy will hear about most)

These do quotes, job booking, scheduling and invoices. They're built mainly for trades doing lots of short jobs (plumbers, electricians, heating engineers), not for project builders with lots of extras.

| Product | Typical price (ex VAT) | Charges by | Good at | Doesn't do (that JobGuard does) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tradify** | £34 / £37 / £44 | per user | Quotes, scheduling, invoices; reading supplier bills on the top plan | Checking supplier invoices against orders and deliveries; building a final account from approved extras; the Final Check |
| **Powered Now** | £28–£40 | per user | Quotes, invoices, forms; receipt scanning on the top plan | Same gaps |
| **Fergus** | £39 / £45 | per month (sources differ) | Job phases, quotes, invoices | Same gaps |
| **YourTradebase** | £29 + £15 per extra user | per month plus users | Simple quotes and invoices | Same gaps |
| **ServiceM8** | £25 / £59 / £119 (more above) | number of jobs a month, unlimited users | High-volume call-out and service work | Built for many short jobs, not long projects with extras |
| **Jobber, Joist** | priced in US dollars | per plan | Quotes and invoices; change orders on higher plans | Same gaps; US-focused |

### 2. Free and cheap quoting tools

- **Checkatrade** gives its members a free quoting and invoicing tool.
- **Voice-to-quote apps** (for example Qted at £29/£59, VoxTrade from free to about £15) turn a spoken description into a quote.

**What to say:** "Quoting's free with JobGuard too. The difference is what happens after the quote: JobGuard runs the job, keeps every extra, checks your merchant invoices, builds your final bill and does the Final Check."

### 3. Big construction software

Buildertrend, Procore, Simpro, Commusoft, BigChange, Causeway, COINS. Powerful, but built for bigger firms. Often thousands of pounds a year, per-user pricing, annual contracts and setup fees. Out of reach or overkill for most builders with 1–20 people.

**What to say:** "Those are built for big contractors with an office team. JobGuard gives a small firm the money protection without the price tag or the setup."

### 4. Accounting software

Xero (£18–£70 a month) and QuickBooks. Builders' accountants use them. **JobGuard doesn't replace them** — it sends the figures across. Position JobGuard as working alongside the builder's accounting software.

### 5. Recovery and chasing services

Debt collectors, credit control software, claims consultants and quantity surveyors. They charge:

| Who | Typical fee |
|---|---|
| Debt collectors, recent business debts | 8–15% of what's collected |
| Debt collectors, old debts | 20–35% |
| Claims firms (capped by the regulator) | 15–30% |
| Firms auditing businesses' supplier payments | usually 20–30% of what's recovered |
| Construction claims consultants | £400–£1,200 a day |

**What to say about the 10%:** "Most people who get paid only when you get paid charge more than 10%. And JobGuard's check is built into the job, so you're not paying a consultant by the day to chase a £500 extra."

## What makes JobGuard different

As of September 2026, no other product we found for small UK builders does these things:

1. **Checks supplier invoices against what was ordered and what was delivered**, and against agreed prices, before the builder pays. Other apps at most read the invoice or match it to the builder's own purchase order.
2. **Builds the final account automatically** from the accepted quote plus approved extras.
3. **Runs a Final Check after the bill is complete** to find work that was done but not billed, using the job's own receipts, photos, notes and messages.
4. **Only charges a fee on money it finds or recovers**, and only once the builder's been paid.
5. **Charges by jobs on the go, not by person**, so the whole team can use it.
6. **Says the job once**, from site-visit talk-through to paid invoice, with nothing retyped.

**Be careful:** "no other product we found" is true for what we checked. Don't say "nobody else in the world does this" — someone might, or might soon.

## How to talk about competitors

- **Never run them down.** Builders who use them chose them for a reason.
- **Ask what they do and don't like** about their current app.
- **Point to the gap:** "Does it check your merchant invoices? Build your final bill from your extras? Tell you what you forgot to bill?"
- **Be honest if a rival suits them better** (for example a high-volume call-out firm on ServiceM8).
- **JobGuard can replace their quote-and-invoice app** for project builders, because it quotes, runs jobs and invoices. It works alongside their accounting software.

## The one thing to remember from Part 7

The other apps help builders **do** the paperwork. JobGuard makes sure the paperwork **gets them paid for everything**. That's the difference to lead with.
