# JobGuard Sales Bible — Part 1: What JobGuard is and how it works

This is part 1 of the JobGuard Sales Bible, written for Tommy, who sells JobGuard. Everything here describes the finished product as builders will buy and use it.

## JobGuard in one sentence

JobGuard is the profit watchdog for builders: you talk through a job once, and JobGuard turns it into the quote, runs the live job, keeps every extra, and builds the final bill for you — then does its own Final Check to find money you did the work for but forgot to bill.

## JobGuard in three sentences (the version for the merchant counter)

Builders lose money in small ways on every job: things missed on the site visit, extras nobody wrote down, supplier invoices nobody checked, photos nobody took, bills sent weeks late. JobGuard catches those as the job goes, so the builder bills everything they did and pays only for what they ordered. Quoting is free, the plans start at £29 a month, and if JobGuard's Final Check finds work you forgot to bill, it only takes 10% of what your customer actually pays you for it.

## The big idea: "Say it once"

Most builders write the same job down four or five times: notes on the site visit, the quote, a list of extras in a notebook or on WhatsApp, the invoice, and something for the accountant. Every time it's rewritten, something falls off.

With JobGuard the job is written down once. The same job carries through from site visit to paid:

1. **Walk it.** On the site visit the builder talks through the job into their phone, like telling a mate what needs doing. JobGuard turns it into a tidy list of work, with every line linked back to what was said.
2. **Quote it.** The builder checks the list, adds prices (JobGuard suggests them from the builder's own rates), and sends a professional quote. Free, always.
3. **Win it.** The customer accepts the quote online. JobGuard records exactly what was agreed and when.
4. **Switch it live.** The builder presses "Switch live". The accepted quote is locked as the baseline — the agreed price nobody can quietly change later.
5. **Run it.** JobGuard watches the job: orders, deliveries, supplier bills, whether the site is ready, whether photos were taken before work was covered. It only asks the builder for decisions that are genuinely theirs.
6. **Log extras as they happen.** When the customer asks for something extra, the builder taps "Log an extra", says or types what it is, prices it, and the customer approves it on their phone. It goes straight onto the final bill. Nothing to remember.
7. **Finish it.** When the work is done, JobGuard has already built the final account: the accepted quote plus every approved extra. The builder reviews it, adds anything else they remember, and presses "Complete final account".
8. **Final Check.** JobGuard then does its own check of the job's receipts, photos, notes and customer messages. If it finds work that was done but isn't on the bill, it shows the builder, with proof. The builder decides whether to add it.
9. **Bill it and get paid.** The invoice goes out the same day, with a pay-now link. JobGuard tracks what's paid, chases what isn't (with the builder's approval), and helps recover money that's being wrongly withheld.

Nothing is typed twice. The quote becomes the job; the job becomes the bill.

## The everyday tour: what a builder actually sees

**The jobs list.** Every job with its stage: quoting, won, live, finished, paid. At the top: "5 of 7 jobs on the go".

**The Decisions inbox.** A short list of things only the builder can decide. For example: "Supplier invoice from Jewson is £84 more than the order — check it", "Kitchen first-fix photos missing before plastering on Thursday", "Mrs Patel hasn't approved the extra for the outside tap". Things that must be dealt with are kept separate from things that are just useful to know. JobGuard doesn't nag about things that don't need the builder.

**Log an extra.** A big button on every live job. Say "Customer wants two extra double sockets in the kitchen", pick or type a price, send it to the customer to approve. Done in under a minute. It's now on the final bill.

**The customer's side.** The builder's customer (usually a homeowner) gets a simple link on their phone to accept the quote, approve extras, and sign off stages. No app to download. This gives the builder written approval for every extra — the thing that wins arguments.

**Proof.** Photo reminders tied to stages ("take photos of the first-fix wiring before the boards go up"). Every photo is dated and linked to the job and the right part of the work.

**Materials.** Snap or forward supplier invoices and delivery notes (or connect a merchant account so they flow in automatically). JobGuard reads them, matches them against the order and the delivery, and puts anything wrong in "Things to check".

**The final account.** Built automatically from the accepted quote plus every approved extra, with the VAT worked out. The builder reviews, adds anything else, and completes it.

**Invoices and payments.** Numbered invoices, credit notes if something needs correcting, stage payments and deposits, pay-now links (card or pay-by-bank), payment tracking, reminders.

**Recovery.** When a customer won't pay money they owe, the builder can open a recovery case. JobGuard puts together the evidence (the accepted quote, the approved extras, the photos, the messages), drafts factual, polite letters for the builder to approve and send, and tracks the case until the money lands.

**The value receipt.** Every month, and for every job, JobGuard shows two separate things: "Extras you logged and we carried through to your bill" (JobGuard's fee on those: £0) and "Money JobGuard's Final Check found after your bill was complete" (the only place a 10% fee can arise).

**The mobile app.** Works on site with no signal — voice notes, photos and extras save on the phone and upload when the signal comes back.

## What JobGuard does NOT do (say this plainly)

- **It never holds anyone's money.** Customer payments go straight to the builder's own account through a regulated payment provider. JobGuard's fees are collected separately.
- **It never sends anything without the builder's say-so.** Quotes, extras, invoices, reminders and recovery letters all need the builder's approval. JobGuard never contacts a customer on its own.
- **It never lets the AI decide anything that matters.** The AI drafts; the builder confirms. The AI doesn't set prices, approve extras, send messages or decide fees.
- **It's not a debt collector, solicitor or claims company.** It helps the builder chase their own money with evidence and factual letters. It doesn't take anyone to court.
- **It doesn't give legal or tax advice.** It handles VAT, CIS and notices on paperwork, but the builder's accountant and solicitor remain their advisers.
- **It doesn't replace accounting software.** It sends the numbers to Xero or QuickBooks; the accountant still uses those.
- **It doesn't sell the builder's data or their customers' details.** Ever.

## Trust and safety in plain English

- **The builder's data stays in the UK and EU**, and belongs to the builder. They can export it.
- **Records are dated and checkable.** Quotes, approvals, extras and photos are saved in a way that shows if anything was changed later. Don't call it "tamper-proof" or "court-ready" — say "a dated, checkable record of the whole job".
- **Signing in** is by a code sent to the builder's email — no passwords to lose.
- **Every user sees only their own company's jobs.**

## The one thing to remember from Part 1

JobGuard is not "another app for quotes and invoices". It's a watchdog on the builder's money that happens to do quotes and invoices, because that's where the money leaks. The builder says the job once; JobGuard makes sure everything they did gets billed, everything they paid for was right, and everything they're owed gets paid.
