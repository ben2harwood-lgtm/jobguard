# JobGuard Sales Bible — Part 3: The Final Check and the 10% fee

This is part 3 of the JobGuard Sales Bible. The Final Check is JobGuard's most distinctive feature and the one most likely to raise questions. Tommy must be able to explain it simply, honestly and confidently. Get this part right and the rest of the sale is easy.

## The deal in one breath

"Everything you log yourself is 100% yours. When you finish your final bill, JobGuard does its own Final Check of your receipts, photos, notes and customer messages. If it finds work you did but didn't bill, it shows you with the proof. If you add it and your customer pays, JobGuard takes 10% of that. If it finds nothing, or your customer doesn't pay, you pay nothing extra."

## Why builders like it

- **Heads you win, tails you still win.** Log everything and you keep everything. Forget something and the Final Check can catch it — you keep 90% of money you'd otherwise have lost.
- **It's a second pair of eyes after you've done your own bill.** Like a proofread. You do your bill first; JobGuard checks it afterwards.
- **You only pay when you get paid.** No fee on anything the customer hasn't actually paid.
- **10% is low.** Debt collectors typically charge 8–15% on recent debts and more on old ones; claims firms 15–30%; firms that audit supplier payments for businesses usually 20–30%.

## How it works, step by step

1. **During the job, JobGuard collects evidence** as normal: supplier receipts and delivery notes, orders, photos, the builder's diary and voice notes, and messages from the customer sent through JobGuard.
2. **The builder logs extras with Log an extra** whenever they remember to. Those are always fee-free.
3. **At the end, JobGuard builds the final account** from the accepted quote plus the approved extras.
4. **The builder reviews it and adds anything else** they remember. Anything added here is also fee-free.
5. **The builder presses "Complete final account"** and confirms: "I've reviewed the work and extras on this job and this is my complete final account." JobGuard saves that bill exactly as it is, with the date and time.
6. **The Final Check runs straight away.** JobGuard compares its evidence against three things: the accepted quote, the extras the builder logged, and the completed final bill. Anything that looks like extra work but appears in none of them is shown to the builder.
7. **The builder sees each item with its proof.** For example:
   > **Outside tap**
   > Customer message, 8 June: "Could you put a tap outside while you're doing the utility?"
   > Merchant receipt, 10 June: outdoor tap kit.
   > Photo, 12 June: tap fitted.
   > Accepted quote: no outside tap. Your extras: none. Your completed final bill: no outside tap.
8. **The builder decides, item by item:**
   - "Yes, this was extra work" → it's priced and approved like any extra, and goes on the invoice.
   - "Already included" → the builder points to the line on the bill that covers it.
   - "In the original quote" → the builder points to the quote line.
   - "Not done", "Not chargeable", or "Wrong job" → it's dismissed. No fee.
   - "I chose not to charge for this" is a perfectly good answer. Builders sometimes do small things free for goodwill. That's their call, and there's no fee.
9. **Nothing is ever charged automatically.** The customer only pays for what the builder bills, and JobGuard only earns 10% of what the customer actually pays for the items the builder confirmed.
10. **Because the Final Check runs before the invoice goes out**, any confirmed items go on the same final invoice. No awkward second bill weeks later.

## What counts as "yours" (always free) and what can earn 10%

| Where the extra came from | JobGuard's fee |
|---|---|
| The builder logged it with **Log an extra** — even a half-finished draft they never priced | **£0** |
| The builder added it at the final review, before completing the final account | **£0** |
| It was in the original accepted quote | **£0** |
| JobGuard had to warn the builder early (see "When JobGuard tells you straight away" below) | **£0** |
| It's only in the builder's **diary or voice notes**, never logged as an extra | **10%** of what's paid for it |
| It's only shown by a **supplier receipt, a photo, or a customer message** | **10%** of what's paid for it |
| The builder dismissed it, didn't bill it, or the customer didn't pay | **£0** |
| Evidence that only arrived after the builder completed the final account | **£0** |

**The simple rule to tell builders:** "If you want to bill it, use Log an extra — then it's 100% yours. Your diary and notes are for your records. The Final Check is the safety net for anything that never made it into Log an extra."

JobGuard shows the same reminder inside the app: "Notes are for your records. To bill for extra work, use Log an extra. JobGuard checks your records against your final bill after you complete it." Nobody is being tricked.

## When JobGuard tells you straight away (and never charges)

JobGuard does not wait for the Final Check when waiting could hurt the builder. It tells them immediately — and those items can **never** earn JobGuard a fee — when:

- **There's a safety issue.**
- **The customer is asking for significant extra work that needs their written agreement before it starts.** Getting that agreement first protects the builder.
- **A dispute is building** and staying quiet would make it worse.
- **There's a legal duty** to tell the builder.

This is written into JobGuard's rules. If there's ever a question of "the builder's interest or JobGuard's fee", the builder's interest wins, and JobGuard gives up the fee.

## The questions builders will ask (and honest answers)

**"So you know about it during the job and don't tell me?"**
Be straight: "Yes — during the job JobGuard notices things, and unless it's urgent it keeps them for the Final Check. That's the deal, and it's in the terms from day one: anything you log is completely free; what our check finds after you've done your own bill costs 10% if you get paid for it. If it's urgent — safety, big work that needs your customer's OK first, or an argument brewing — we tell you straight away and never charge."

Never deny it, never dodge it. Builders respect a straight answer, and the whole deal only works if they trust it.

**"Why not just tell me as soon as you spot it?"**
"Because then you'd be paying for JobGuard to do your job for you on every little thing. The way it works, everything you capture yourself is free — so you're always in control of what you pay. The Final Check is a second opinion after you've done your own bill. And anything urgent, we tell you straight away."

**"What if I'd have remembered it anyway?"**
"Then you'd have added it before completing your final bill, and it'd have been free. The Final Check only shows things that weren't on the bill you completed."

**"What stops you inventing things to charge me for?"**
"We can't charge you for anything. You decide whether each item is real. You bill it or you don't. And we only get 10% of what your customer actually pays for it."

**"What if my customer refuses to pay for it?"**
"Then there's no fee. We only earn when you get paid."

**"What if the customer pays and then gets some back?"**
"The fee follows the money. If they get a refund, our fee goes down with it and we credit you the difference."

**"Is this on top of the monthly plan?"**
"Yes. The monthly plan pays for everything JobGuard does all month — the checks, the quotes, the extras, the invoices, the proof. The 10% is only on extra money the Final Check finds. They're separate, and one never cancels out the other."

**"10% sounds like a lot."**
"It's 10% of money you'd otherwise never have seen. You keep 90%. Debt collectors charge around 8–15% on recent debts, and more on old ones. And anything you log yourself is always 100% yours."

**"Do I have to use the Final Check?"**
"It runs when you complete your final account. You always decide what to do with anything it finds — including ignoring it."

## Other things that can earn JobGuard 10%

The Final Check is the main one. There are two others, both only on cash actually received:

1. **Supplier overcharge refunded in cash.** If the builder has already paid a supplier too much and JobGuard helps get a cash refund, JobGuard takes 10% of the refund. Overcharges caught **before** paying are free. Credit notes against future orders are free.
2. **Money a customer is wrongly holding back, recovered through a recovery case.** Only when the builder chooses to open a case and agrees the fee for that case up front. Normal reminders and ordinary late payment are never charged.

## What never earns JobGuard a fee

- Anything the builder logs or adds themselves
- Anything in the original quote
- Overcharges caught before the builder pays
- Credit notes
- Things JobGuard had to warn about early
- Money not yet received, or pending
- Normal late payments and reminders
- Anything the builder decides not to bill
- Anything based on the AI's opinion alone — every item needs evidence and the builder's confirmation

## How the fee is shown and collected

When the customer's payment comes in, the builder sees it on one screen:
> £800 received for Final Check items · JobGuard fee £80 + VAT · Yours £720

The fee is collected by Direct Debit shortly afterwards, not on a surprise invoice weeks later. JobGuard never takes it out of the customer's payment — that goes straight to the builder.

## Words to use and words to avoid

**Use:** "Final Check", "second pair of eyes after your bill", "safety net", "you keep 90%", "only when you get paid", "anything you log is 100% yours", "shown with the proof".

**Avoid:**
- "Hidden", "secret", "we hold it back" — they're not lies, but they set the wrong tone. If a builder asks directly whether JobGuard knows during the job, answer honestly (see above).
- "You'd have forgotten this" or "you'd never have billed this" — we can't know that.
- "JobGuard saved you £X" — say "JobGuard found £X and you've been paid £X".
- "Guaranteed" anything.
- "Claims" or "debt collection" — JobGuard is neither.

## The one thing to remember from Part 3

Always explain the Final Check and the 10% **before** a builder signs up, in plain words, every time. A builder who hears it from Tommy first sees a fair deal. A builder who finds out later feels caught out, and tells everyone at the merchant's.
