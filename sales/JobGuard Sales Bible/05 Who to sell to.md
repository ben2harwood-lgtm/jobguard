# JobGuard Sales Bible — Part 5: Who to sell to

This is part 5 of the JobGuard Sales Bible. It describes the builders JobGuard is built for, how to spot a good prospect, where to find them, and who isn't a fit.

## The ideal customer in one line

A UK builder doing project work — extensions, loft conversions, kitchens, bathrooms, renovations, refurbishments — for homeowners or small businesses, with anywhere from 1 to about 20 people, who does extras on most jobs and does the paperwork in the evenings.

**Sweet spot:** firms of 2–10 people running 3–15 jobs at once. They have enough jobs for things to slip, and no office manager to catch them.

## The three builder types

### 1. The sole trader ("Solo")

- **Who:** one person, maybe a labourer now and then. Bathrooms, kitchens, small extensions, general building. About £100k–£250k a year of work, 2–4 jobs at once.
- **Their day:** on the tools all day, quotes and invoices at the kitchen table at night. Extras agreed on the doorstep and remembered (or not) at the end.
- **What hurts:** forgetting extras, chasing the last payment, admin eating their evenings, Making Tax Digital forcing them onto software.
- **What they want to hear:** "Talk through the job on site, it writes the quote. Log extras in seconds. Your bill's ready the day you finish. £29 a month, about a pound a day. First job free."
- **Plan:** Solo.

### 2. The small firm ("Builder")

- **Who:** 3–8 people, often the owner plus a partner or spouse doing the books. Extensions, loft conversions, full refurbishments. About £400k–£1m a year, 5–8 jobs at once.
- **Their day:** the owner runs between sites, prices new work, orders materials. The partner does invoices and chases payments. Extras get agreed by whoever's on site and don't always get passed on.
- **What hurts:** extras agreed on site that never reach the bill, merchant invoices nobody checks, days lost because materials weren't there, disputes with no photos, cash flow.
- **What they want to hear:** "Everyone on site logs extras the same way, and the customer approves them on their phone. The partner doing the books sees everything in one place. Supplier invoices are checked before you pay. £69 a month for the whole team, not per person."
- **Plan:** Builder. This is the plan most builders should end up on.
- **Important:** sell to both the owner and the person who does the books. Often the partner is the one who feels the pain and makes the decision.

### 3. The bigger firm (contractor deal)

- **Who:** 10–20 people, possibly an office manager or bookkeeper, possibly working as a subcontractor for main contractors as well as for homeowners. About £1.5m–£4m a year, 10–20 jobs at once.
- **Their day:** site managers run jobs; the office handles orders, applications for payment and invoices; the owner watches margins.
- **What hurts:** margin leaking across many jobs with no single view; commercial paperwork (applications for payment, pay-less notices, retention); subcontract disputes; slow or withheld payments from main contractors; checking dozens of supplier invoices a month.
- **What they want to hear:** "One view of every job's money. Every extra approved in writing. Supplier invoices checked automatically. Applications, notices and retention tracked with deadlines. Evidence packs ready if there's a dispute."
- **Deal:** the contractor deal (10% of site-logged extras once paid, no monthly fee by default). Any firm with more than 7 jobs at once goes on it. Pass these to Ben until the contractor edition of this bible is ready.

## Signs of a great prospect

Listen for these in conversation:

- "I'm sure I do extras I never charge for."
- "The customer said they thought that was included."
- "I haven't checked a merchant invoice properly in years."
- "My wife/husband/partner does the books and it's a nightmare."
- "I do my paperwork on Sunday nights."
- "I'm still owed money from a job last year."
- "We had to open up a wall because nobody took a photo."
- "I need to get software for Making Tax Digital anyway."
- "We're growing and I'm losing track."
- They quote from memory or a notebook, or use WhatsApp for extras.

## Moments when builders are ready to buy

- Just after a job where they lost money on extras or a dispute
- When a customer refuses to pay the last bit
- When they take on their first employee or second van
- When Making Tax Digital forces them to choose software
- January–February and the winter generally: planning for the year, time to set things up
- When they're unhappy with their current app, or its per-user price rises as they grow

## Who is NOT a good fit (don't waste time, don't oversell)

- **Emergency call-out trades doing several small jobs a day with no extras** (for example a drain unblocker or a locksmith). A booking and dispatch app such as ServiceM8 suits them better. Be honest and say so.
- **Big contractors with hundreds of jobs** are not a fit for the monthly plans, but they are JobGuard's main target on the contractor deal. Pass them straight to Ben until the contractor edition of this bible is ready.
- **Builders who genuinely never do extras**, and invoice one fixed price per job with nothing added. They'll get less from JobGuard.
- **Anyone who won't use a phone app at all.** JobGuard needs the builder to at least take photos and log extras on their phone.
- **Anyone who wants JobGuard to chase or pressure their customers for them automatically.** JobGuard never contacts customers without the builder's approval, and it's not a debt collector.

## Where to find them

- **Builders' merchants**, early morning at the trade counter (Travis Perkins, Jewson, Selco, local independents). That's where builders are. Note: JobGuard checks merchants' invoices, so it never takes money from merchants or does deals with them. Meet builders there; don't partner with the merchant.
- **Trade shows and local trade events.**
- **Trade associations:** the Federation of Master Builders (FMB), the National Federation of Builders, local builders' groups.
- **Online:** Facebook groups for builders and tradespeople, trade forums, YouTube and Instagram trade communities.
- **Accountants and bookkeepers who look after builders.** They see the mess every year-end and are pushing clients onto software for Making Tax Digital. They make good referral partners. (Ask Ben about any referral scheme.)
- **Existing customers.** Builders know builders. Ask every happy customer: "Who else do you know who forgets to charge for extras?"
- **Subcontractors on sites.** Firms using JobGuard can invite their subcontractors, who see how it works.

## The buyer and the influencers

- **The decision maker** is almost always the owner.
- **The person who feels the pain** is often the one doing the books: a partner, spouse, or office manager. Get them in the room.
- **The accountant** influences software choices, especially with Making Tax Digital coming in.
- **The site manager or foreman** decides whether it actually gets used on site. For bigger firms, show them Log an extra and the photo reminders.

## The one thing to remember from Part 5

Sell to builders who do extras, have more than one job going, and hate paperwork. That's most builders doing project work. Be honest when someone isn't a fit: a builder you turn away politely today will recommend you tomorrow.
