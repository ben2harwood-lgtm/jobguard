# Success-fee / contingency pricing benchmarks for a 10% JobGuard success fee

Research date: 2026-09-24. Scope: what SMEs (especially UK construction firms) are used to paying when a third party finds or recovers money for them, to benchmark JobGuard's proposed 10% success fee (`reference_fee_policy_v3`, proposed) on (a) missed extras found after the builder's final bill, (b) supplier overcharge refunds, (c) withheld customer payments recovered.

Source-quality note: many fee figures below come from vendor blogs or pricing-guide pages written by firms in the same market (content marketing). They are useful as statements of market norms but are not independent surveys. Where a figure comes from a single vendor or an AI-style summary site, it is marked **[single source / unverified]**. No independent, published UK survey of contingency-fee levels across these sectors was found.

## 1. Recovery audit / profit recovery firms (AP recovery, duplicate payments)

### Takeaway
Accounts-payable recovery audit is almost universally contingency-priced; published ranges cluster around 20-30% of recovered value, with outer bounds of roughly 10-40%. UK boutique firms advertise "no recovery, no fee" but do not publish their percentages. On this benchmark, 10% sits at the very bottom of the range.

### Cited Findings
- PRGX (largest global recovery-audit firm) says it earns most of its revenue from contingent fees: a pre-agreed percentage of the successful claims or recoveries its audits produce. Its contracts set the fee as a percentage of amounts the client recovers from overpayment claims. — [PRGX 10-K FY2019 (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1007330/000100733020000003/a201910kprgx.htm)
- PRGX describes its AP recovery audits as "self-funding or contingency-based": the fee is tied to a percentage of recovered funds, and nothing is owed if nothing is recovered. — [PRGX AP profit recovery](https://www.prgx.com/solutions/finance-shared-services/ap-profit-recoveries/)
- Contingency recovery-audit fees are "often ranging between 20 to 30 percent", depending on complexity, transaction volume and how much history is reviewed. Some auditors charge 10%-40%. — [apexanalytix blog, "How Much Does an AP Recovery Audit Cost?"](https://www.apexanalytix.com/resources/blog/recovery-audit-cost/) (via search snippet; the full page failed to load, so this is **[unverified in full text]**. apexanalytix is itself a recovery-audit vendor.)
- AP recovery audits "typically recover $1M for every $1B in supplier spend". — [PRGX AP recovery audit guide](https://www.prgx.com/guides/ap-recovery-audit-services-guide/) (via search snippet; vendor claim)
- UK firms Westgate Moore ("NO RECOVERY – NO FEE") and Contingency Recovery Associates (c-r-a.co.uk) offer contingency AP audits but publish no percentage. Prospects have to contact them. — [Westgate Moore](https://www.westgatemoore.co.uk/payments-audit/); [Contingency Recovery Associates](https://www.c-r-a.co.uk/accounts-payable-audit)
- Other UK duplicate-payment and AP recovery providers that appeared in searches (fees not checked): Twice2Much, duplicatepayments.co.uk, Fiscal Technologies, and Liberata on the public-sector G-Cloud marketplace. — [Twice2Much](https://www.twice2much.com/); [Liberata G-Cloud listing](https://www.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud/services/845673780445211)

### Inferences
- The recovery-audit market sells to mid-size and large AP departments. The 20-30% norm pays for a labour-intensive human audit of years of ledger data. JobGuard's software-driven detection costs less per unit, which supports a lower percentage.
- A 10% fee is clearly below the recovery-audit norm. Anyone with a procurement or finance background will likely see it as cheap, not greedy.

### Gaps
- I found no published percentages from UK AP recovery boutiques (Westgate Moore, CRA, Twice2Much, Meridian, Leaf, "Cost Recovery Services"). Their fees are quote-only.
- The apexanalytix figures could not be checked against the full article.
- I found nothing about recovery-audit services sold to micro or small construction firms specifically.

## 2. Supplier-invoice, utility, telecom and freight bill audit / cost-reduction consultancies

### Takeaway
Cost-reduction and bill-audit consultancies commonly charge about 50% of the savings or refunds they find, sometimes for multi-year periods. This is the most expensive familiar benchmark and makes 10% look modest. The adjacent UK energy-broker model is hidden commission per kWh rather than a visible percentage, and that model is now under regulatory and legal pressure because of non-disclosure.

### Cited Findings
- P3 Cost Analysts (US-led cost-reduction franchise) works on contingency: no fee if unsuccessful, and if it finds savings they are shared "50/50 for a finite period of time (from 12-60 months)". Most clients save 15%-30%. — [P3 Cost Analysts](https://www.costanalysts.com/)
- Inspired PLC (UK) runs a "Forensic Telecoms Cost Audit" covering up to six years of bills. It claims more than 2,000 telecom audits and over £265m in refunds and ongoing savings. The fee basis was not shown in the result. — [Inspired PLC telecoms cost audit](https://inspiredplc.co.uk/how-we-help/telecoms/cost-audit/)
- Other UK and US utility/telecom audit firms that surfaced (fees not checked): Utility Auditors (UK), Cost Reduction Services (UK), Cost Control Associates, Waste Consultants Inc. — [Utility Auditors](https://www.utilityauditors.co.uk/); [Cost Reduction Services](https://www.costreductionservices.co.uk/services/)
- UK SME energy brokers usually earn 0.3-2.0p/kWh added to the unit rate, paid by the supplier over the contract. A "good" SME rate is said to be 1-2p/kWh, and anything above 3p/kWh should be questioned. — [Connection Technologies, broker fees 2026](https://connection-technologies.co.uk/blog/business-energy-broker-fees-explained); [Meet George, broker commission guide](https://meetgeorge.co.uk/blog/energy-broker-commission-rates-guide) (broker-industry sources)
- Since October 2024, Ofgem has required commission disclosure by third-party intermediaries in the non-domestic market. DESNZ confirmed (October 2025) that Ofgem will authorise brokers under a new TPI regime around 2027-28. — [Crown Gas & Power summary of government response](https://www.crowngasandpower.co.uk/government-confirms-ofgem-to-regulate-energy-brokers-and-tpis/); [Ofgem, Businesses' experiences of the energy market 2025 (May 2026)](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-05/Businesses'-experiences-of-the-energy-market-report-2025.pdf) (secondary summary. I did not read the Ofgem report in full.)
- In *Expert Tooling and Automation Ltd v Engie Power Ltd* [2025] EWCA Civ 292, the Court of Appeal held that "half-secret" broker commissions can breach fiduciary duty. — reported in the same search results ([British Energy Compliance procurement guide](https://britishenergycompliance.co.uk/blog/business-energy-procurement-guide-2026/)) **[secondary source; judgment not read]**

### Inferences
- SMEs broadly accept "we share what we save you", but the energy-broker story shows that hidden or poorly disclosed intermediary fees cause backlash and regulation. JobGuard's 10% should be shown openly at the point each fee arises, which fits the D11/D13 fairness posture already in the plan.
- 50/50 savings shares usually apply to ongoing savings over months or years, not one-off refunds. That is a different base from JobGuard's one-off recovered principal, so compare the headline percentages with care.

### Gaps
- No UK freight-audit fee data was found.
- No published UK utility/telecom audit percentages were found (Inspired, Utility Auditors and others quote on request). The "25-50%" range in the brief is supported only by P3's 50%. Treat the lower end as **[unverified]**.

## 3. UK commercial debt collection agencies (and the Late Payment Act)

### Takeaway
UK B2B collection agencies charge on a no-collection-no-fee basis. Rates are about 8-15% for fresh (under 90 days), well-documented invoices, rising to 20-35% for aged or distressed debt. Some firms advertise from 5%. JobGuard's 10% on withheld customer payments therefore matches what a builder would pay an agency for a fresh debt, with detection and evidence assembly included. The Late Payment Act lets a creditor add statutory interest and fixed compensation, but only against business debtors, not homeowners.

### Cited Findings
- UK B2B collection is mostly no-win-no-fee, with agency commission of 8-25% of the amount actually recovered. — [Cosmo DCA, "Debt Collection Agency Charges UK" (21 Mar 2026)](https://www.cosmodca.com/blog/debt-collection-agency-charges-uk)
- By debt age: fresh, well-documented commercial invoices under 90 days old, 8-15%; aged files of 12-24 months, 20-25% (recovery probability about 40%); distressed files over 3 years, 25-35%. — [Cosmo DCA (2026)](https://www.cosmodca.com/blog/debt-collection-agency-charges-uk)
- Alternative model: a £25-75 file-opening fee plus a lower success commission of 6-10%. The break-even for placing a single invoice is about £1,500-2,000 and up. Court, solicitor, HCEO enforcement and tracing costs are separate pass-through costs outside the commission. — [Cosmo DCA (2026)](https://www.cosmodca.com/blog/debt-collection-agency-charges-uk)
- Some UK agencies advertise commission "from 5%" for UK-based debts. Another advertises no-win-no-fee at 10-25% of the amount recovered. — search-result summaries of [Creditreform UK](https://creditreform.co.uk/debt-collection-agency/no-win-no-fee-debt-collection/), [Athena Collections](https://www.athenacollections.co.uk/pricing-debtcollectionfeesuk/), [debtcollection.co.uk](https://www.debtcollection.co.uk/services/no-win-no-fee-no-up-front-charges/) **[attribution of each figure to a specific firm not verified; pages not fetched]**
- Claimed context: the average commercial debt write-off per SME is £6,000 (2025), and agencies recover 60-70% on aged debt. — search summary, source page unclear (likely [Garfield AI help page](https://www.garfield.law/help/how-much-do-debt-collectors-charge) or Cosmo DCA) **[unverified]**
- Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998: statutory interest at Bank of England base rate + 8%. Fixed compensation paid by the debtor is £40 (under £1,000), £70 (£1,000-£9,999) or £100 (£10,000+). These amounts lower the creditor's net cost of collection. — [Cosmo DCA (2026)](https://www.cosmodca.com/blog/debt-collection-agency-charges-uk)
- GOV.UK frames the late-payment rights as applying when "another business" pays late: B2B payment terms are usually a maximum of 60 days, and payment is late 30 days after invoice or delivery if no date was agreed. — [GOV.UK, Late commercial payments](https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery)

### Inferences
- Most of a small builder's customers are homeowners, so the Late Payment Act (B2B only) will usually give no statutory interest or compensation to offset JobGuard's fee on recovered customer payments. The builder bears the 10% in full. For commercial and main-contractor customers, the fixed compensation (£40-£100) partly offsets it.
- Withheld final payments on building jobs are fresh (days to weeks old), evidenced debts, which is the 8-15% agency band. 10% is in line with the market there and competitive once JobGuard's evidence packs are taken into account. It would look expensive only against an agency's "from 5%" tier or the fixed-fee-plus-6-10% model.
- Agencies' percentages exclude legal and enforcement costs. JobGuard should say clearly whether its 10% covers any escalation, or whether escalation is out of scope.

### Gaps
- No CICM (Chartered Institute of Credit Management) or CSA (Credit Services Association) published fee benchmark was found. The CSA mainly represents consumer-debt collectors.
- The exact statutory wording on recovering "reasonable costs" above the fixed sum (s.5A of the 1998 Act) was not checked against legislation.gov.uk.

## 4. Construction claims consultants, QSs and adjudication services

### Takeaway
UK construction claims consultants and QSs mostly charge day rates (about £350-£1,200 per day) or fixed fees. No-win-no-fee is offered case by case. The legal ceiling for a lawyer's damages-based agreement in a commercial or construction claim is 50% of the sum recovered, including VAT. I found no published "standard" contingency percentage for final-account or variation recovery. Against day-rate pricing, JobGuard's 10% with no upfront cost is structurally cheaper for small-value missed extras.

### Cited Findings
- UK construction claims consultants "typically charge between £400 and £1,200 per day" depending on seniority and complexity. An outsourced QS for a subcontractor typically costs £350-£800 per day or a monthly retainer. — [RKA Associates construction claims FAQ](https://rkaassociates.com/construction-claims-faq)
- RKA Associates gives bespoke fee proposals after a free initial consultation and does not publish contingency rates. — [RKA Associates](https://rkaassociates.com/construction-claims-faq)
- Specialist construction law firms say they act in adjudications, including final-account recovery, on a no-win-no-fee basis. Consultants' fees are described as "usually a relatively small percentage" of the amount recovered. — [Helix Law, Final Account Recovery](https://helix-law.co.uk/construction-law/final-account-recovery/) (via search summary; exact wording **[unverified]**)
- Damages-Based Agreements Regulations 2013, reg. 4: for non-PI claims at first instance, a DBA payment must not exceed "50% of the sums ultimately recovered by the client", including VAT. The PI cap is 25% including VAT. Employment matters are carved out of this regulation. — [legislation.gov.uk, SI 2013/609 reg. 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/609/regulation/4)
- A commentary for in-house lawyers uses a "30 percent DBA" as its example for a mid-value commercial dispute. It notes that below the 50% cap there is "no guidance" on the "right" level for a given type of case. — [Financier Worldwide, UK contingency fees](https://www.financierworldwide.com/uk-contingency-fees-a-users-guide-for-the-in-house-lawyer)
- Other construction dispute consultancies (e.g. RJH Consulting) offer claims and adjudication services on a quoted basis. — [RJH Consulting](https://www.rjhconsulting.co.uk/dispute-resolution)

### Inferences
- A small builder recovering a £500-£3,000 missed variation could never economically hire a claims consultant at £400-£1,200 per day. JobGuard's 10% (£50-£300 on those sums) opens up a recovery channel that does not otherwise exist for small jobs.
- The legal-DBA reference points (30% illustrative, 50% cap) are litigation or adjudication prices, which involve adversarial work and risk. JobGuard's missed-extras catch is mostly administrative: the builder confirms the extra and bills it. That makes a much lower percentage defensible.

### Gaps
- No UK construction claims consultant publishing a standard contingency percentage for variations or final accounts was found.
- No data was found on payment-adjudication services priced specifically for small domestic builders (the Construction Act adjudication regime generally excludes residential occupiers anyway, which is **[not verified here]**).

## 5. Tax / R&D-credit claim firms and similar "only paid if you get money" references

### Takeaway
UK R&D tax-credit advisers are the most familiar SME "no win no fee" reference. Boutiques charge 15-30% of the benefit, accountancy practices 5-15%, and software-led tools 5-10%. A 10% fee sits at the low end of what SMEs have been conditioned to pay, in the same band as software-assisted claim tools.

### Cited Findings
- UK R&D adviser fees by type: boutique consultancies 15-30%, accountancy practices 5-15% (larger firms with dedicated R&D teams may charge boutique-level rates), software/online platforms 5-10%. — [Myriad Associates, "How Much Should You Be Paying Your R&D Tax Adviser?" (2025)](https://www.myriadassociates.com/resources/news/2025/how-much-should-you-be-paying-your-rd-tax-advisor/)
- A "reasonable" 2025 R&D claim costs 10-25% of the benefit on contingency, or £5,000-£15,000 on a fixed fee. — [Myriad Associates, "How Much Should an R&D Tax Credit Claim Cost in 2025?"](https://www.myriadassociates.com/resources/news/2025/how-much-should-an-rd-tax-credit-claim-cost-in-2025/) (via search summary)
- Examples of individual providers: a 15% + VAT success fee payable when HMRC releases the claim, and a fixed-fee model with a £2,750 + VAT minimum for benefits up to £55,000. — search summaries of [Sleek UK](https://sleek.com/uk/rd-tax-credits/) and [Tax Cloud pricing](https://www.taxcloud.co.uk/pricing/) **[attribution of each figure to a specific firm not verified]**
- Contracts often set aside 2-3% for the client's own accountant, and HMRC's tougher scrutiny in 2025 raises the risk of clawback and penalties (up to 100% for deliberate errors). — search summary of [AccountingWEB thread](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/any-answers/r-d-tax-credit-agent-fees) / [GrowthPad](https://www.growthpad.co.uk/blog/how-much-does-an-r-and-d-tax-claim-really-cost-in-the-uk/) **[unverified attribution]**

### Inferences
- The R&D-credit market, where some firms charged high contingencies on weak claims, damaged SME trust in "no win no fee" advisers. JobGuard should stress that its fee applies only to settled, allocated, unreversed cash (per §5.6/§14.6-14.9). This is a real point of difference from advisers paid on HMRC acceptance that may later be clawed back.
- Business-rates appeal and duty/VAT reclaim agents are also familiar no-win-no-fee references, but I could not find sourced percentages for them.

### Gaps
- No sourced percentages were found for UK business-rates appeal agents, duty-drawback or VAT-reclaim contingency firms (search returned no fee data).
- No independent survey (e.g. from HMRC, ICAEW or the FSB) of R&D agent fee levels was found. The Myriad figures come from an R&D adviser.

## 6. SaaS combining subscription and success fee; customer perception

### Takeaway
Pure success-fee SaaS exists at 25% (Chargeflow, chargeback recovery). Failed-payment recovery SaaS (ProfitWell/Paddle Retain) takes about 10-15% of recovered revenue for larger customers, with flat fees for smaller ones. UK credit-control SaaS (Chaser, Satago) uses flat subscriptions. Chaser layers a separate collections service on top, but I could not find its commission. Pairing a subscription with a separate 10% success fee is uncommon but not unusual. At 10% JobGuard sits at the lower end of recovery-SaaS percentages, and it charges only on money that would otherwise not have been billed or collected.

### Cited Findings
- Chargeflow: "25% Per Recovered Chargeback" with no upfront payment, no contract or minimum on standard plans, and a "4x ROI Guarantee". Separately, $29 per deflected chargeback (Alerts). Enterprise pricing is custom. — [Chargeflow pricing](https://www.chargeflow.io/pricing)
- A third-party comparison notes the 25% does not include the card-network dispute fee. Example: a merchant recovering $15,000 a month pays $3,750 a month. — [Redo, Chargeflow pricing](https://redo.com/resources/articles/comparisons/chargeflow-pricing) (via search summary)
- ProfitWell Retain (Paddle) is performance-based for larger companies, reported at about 10-15% of recovered revenue, and flat-fee for smaller ones (from $500 a month), per Paddle's Help Center (July 2026). — search summary citing [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/customer-success/profitwell-retain-profile/) and related review sites **[10-15% figure is third-party reporting; Paddle help page not fetched]**
- Several competitors now sell flat-fee alternatives to ProfitWell Retain's percentage model. That suggests some customers push back on percentage-of-recovery pricing once recoveries grow. — [SubRevival, "Paddle Retain alternatives: 5 flat-fee tools"](https://subrevival.com/alternatives/profitwell-retain-alternative); [ChurnRecovery comparison](https://churnrecovery.com/compare/profitwell) (competitor marketing; perception evidence is indirect)
- Satago (UK credit control plus invoice finance) charges three fixed tiers at £45, £80 and £200 a month. Chaser prices by company revenue: £199 a month under £4m, £599 under £10m, £899 under £100m, plus a specialist add-on at £900-£2,099 a month. — search summary of [Chaser blog, best credit management software 2026](https://www.chaserhq.com/blog/best-credit-management-software) / [Paidnice, credit control software pricing](https://www.paidnice.com/blog/credit-control-software-pricing) **[vendor-published comparison; not fetched]**
- Chaser is described as the only tool on its own list that combines credit-control software with its own collections service. Its collections commission was not found. — [Chaser, debt collection software 2026](https://www.chaserhq.com/blog/debt-collection-software)

### Inferences
- The "subscription + success fee" hybrid is closest to Chaser's (subscription + collections service) and to Retain's hybrid (flat for small customers, percentage for large). JobGuard's v3 model (subscription D09 + 10% success fee, with no subscription offset) fits established patterns.
- The evidence on perception (flat-fee competitors attacking percentage models, guarantees such as Chargeflow's "4x ROI") suggests that success fees are accepted when the customer sees the recovery as found money they would not otherwise have had, and are resented when the vendor takes a cut of money the customer thinks it would have got anyway. This strongly supports §5.14's rule that builder captures are always fee-free and only JobGuard-originated finds can earn a fee.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of SME attitudes to success-fee SaaS was found.
- Upflow and Satago do not appear to charge success fees (not confirmed). Chaser's collections commission rate was not found.
- No UK construction-SaaS with a success-fee model was identified.

## 7. UK regulatory constraints on collecting / recovering (FCA consumer credit, claims management)

### Takeaway
Collecting a builder's ordinary unpaid invoice from a homeowner does not appear to be FCA-regulated "debt collecting", which is tied to debts under consumer credit or consumer hire agreements. Construction payment recovery is not one of the six regulated claims-management sectors. Risk points remain: payment plans that amount to credit, consumer-protection rules on fair collection, and the DBA rules if a solicitor or claims route is used.

### Cited Findings
- Debt collecting under Article 39F of the Regulated Activities Order is a credit-related regulated activity. — [FCA Handbook, "debt collecting" glossary](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/glossary/G3204); [FCA Handbook SUP 16 Annex 38B](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/sup16/sup16s81)
- The SRA's guidance says regulated debt collecting applies only to recovery under a consumer credit agreement (Article 39F RAO), so recovering unpaid invoices for services provided (e.g. for a building company) is not regulated debt collecting. — [SRA, consumer credit activities guidance](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/consumer-credit-activities/) (via search summary; this reading of the building-company example comes from the summarised result, **[verify wording]**)
- Regulated claims-management activity (RAO arts. 89G-89M) covers lead generation plus six sectors: personal injury, financial services/products, housing disrepair, specified benefits, criminal injury, and employment. — [FCA, Claims management companies: our regulation](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/claims-management-regulation); [legislation.gov.uk, RAO Part 3B](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544/article/89N)
- DBA fee caps (50% commercial, 25% PI, VAT-inclusive) apply to legal representatives' damages-based agreements in claims or proceedings at first instance. — [legislation.gov.uk, SI 2013/609 reg. 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/609/regulation/4)
- The Late Payment Act's statutory interest and compensation are framed for business-to-business debts only (see §3). — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery)

### Inferences
- JobGuard's model helps the builder bill and collect its own invoices. It does not take assignment of debts or collect in its own name from consumers, and the builder, not JobGuard, owns the customer relationship. That keeps it outside Article 39F and the claims-management sectors on the evidence found. This is consistent with the plan's existing regulated-activity deferral, but it needs legal sign-off, not an agent inference (AGENTS.md §1).
- If JobGuard ever lets builders offer homeowners instalment or deferred payment, it could move into consumer credit territory (credit brokerage or debt collecting under a credit agreement). That would need a separate decision.
- "Housing disrepair" is a regulated CMC sector, but it covers tenant claims against landlords. It should not catch builder payment recovery, although marketing copy should avoid wording that sounds like claims management to consumers.

### Gaps
- The exemption for fixed-sum credit repayable in 12 or fewer instalments within 12 months without interest (RAO art. 60F), relevant to staged payment plans, was not checked.
- Consumer-protection obligations on collecting from homeowners (Consumer Rights Act, CPUT/DMCC Act unfair commercial practices) were not researched.
- No legal commentary specific to software platforms taking a percentage of recovered construction payments was found.

Overall positioning (inference): 10% is at or below the bottom of almost every "we only get paid if you get paid" reference an SME builder is likely to know. It roughly equals fresh-debt agency commission and software-led R&D claim fees, is about half to a third of recovery-audit and chargeback-SaaS rates, and is about a fifth of cost-reduction savings shares. The main perception risk is not the level but the base: charging on money the builder feels it would have billed anyway. §5.14's fee-free treatment of all builder captures addresses that directly.
